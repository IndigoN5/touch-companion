User Manual

Everything you need to know about using the Small Business Intelligence dashboard.

Welcome to NUVABASE Small Business Intelligence. This dashboard provides a comprehensive view of your business health, financial performance, growth opportunities, and financing options.

Navigate between sections using the tabs at the top of the page. Each tab focuses on a specific aspect of your business intelligence:

Business Health gives you an at-a-glance overview of key performance indicators. API Connections lets you link external data sources. Financial Performance shows detailed monthly data. Growth Analysis helps plan expansion. SBA & Financing explores funding options. ROI Calculator projects your return on consulting investment. Generate Proposal creates professional documents.

This dashboard runs entirely in your browser with no external dependencies. All data is stored locally and never transmitted to external servers.

Annual Revenue: Total income generated over the trailing 12 months. The year-over-year (YoY) percentage shows growth compared to the previous period.

Profit Margin: Net income as a percentage of revenue. Higher margins indicate more efficient operations and stronger pricing power.

Employee Count: Current full-time equivalent staff. Growth in headcount typically signals business expansion and capacity building.

Customer Retention: Percentage of customers who continue doing business with you year over year. Above 85% is considered strong for professional services.

Cash Runway: Number of months of operating expenses covered by current cash reserves. 3-6 months is a healthy range for small businesses.

Growth Score: A composite metric (0-100) combining revenue trajectory, margin trends, retention rates, and market position. Scores above 70 indicate strong growth momentum.

The API Connections tab allows you to connect external financial platforms to pull live data into the dashboard.

QuickBooks Online: Enter your Client ID, Client Secret, and Realm ID from the QuickBooks Developer portal. This enables automatic sync of revenue, expenses, and P&L data.

SBA.gov: Obtain an API key from the SBA developer portal to access real-time program eligibility and rate data.

Dun & Bradstreet: Connect with your API key and DUNS number to pull business credit scores and competitive intelligence.

Square/Stripe: Enter your payment processor API key to import transaction data and customer purchasing patterns.

CSV Upload: If API connections are not available, you can upload CSV files with your financial data as a fallback option. Files should include columns for date, amount, and category.

All credentials are stored securely in your browser's localStorage and are never transmitted externally.

The Financial Performance tab displays a 12-month detailed breakdown of your business finances.

Revenue: Total income received each month. Look for consistent upward trends and seasonal patterns.

Expenses: Total operating costs. Monitor the revenue-to-expense ratio for efficiency gains.

Profit: Revenue minus expenses. The summary row shows your annual total.

Margin: Profit as a percentage of revenue. Improving margins indicate better operational efficiency or pricing power.

Expense Breakdown: The pie chart shows where your money goes across six categories: Payroll, Materials, Overhead, Marketing, Insurance, and Other. This helps identify areas for cost optimization.

Key Metrics: Revenue growth rate, burn rate, and break-even analysis provide strategic context for financial decisions.

The SBA & Financing tab provides a comprehensive view of your current financing and available SBA programs.

Current Financing: Shows outstanding loans, lines of credit, balances, rates, and payment progress.

Available Programs: Lists SBA programs you may be eligible for, including maximum amounts, rate ranges, terms, and eligibility status.

Eligibility Key: A green checkmark means you meet the basic criteria. A yellow warning means additional review is needed to confirm eligibility.

DSCR: The Debt Service Coverage Ratio measures your ability to service debt. Lenders typically require 1.25x or higher. Your current 2.4x is well above this threshold.

Financing Recommendations: Based on your business metrics, the system suggests optimal financing strategies including primary loan types, credit line adjustments, and supplemental programs.

The ROI Calculator projects the financial impact of a NUVABASE consulting engagement on your business.

Input your current metrics: Annual revenue, profit margin, employee count, and operational waste percentage. The calculator uses these as baseline figures.

Set growth estimates: Enter projected revenue growth, margin improvement, and waste reduction percentages based on your goals.

Results update automatically as you change any input field. The three main impact areas are:

1. Revenue Growth Impact: The dollar value of projected revenue increase.

2. Margin Improvement: Additional profit from improved margins applied to current revenue.

3. Operational Savings: Savings from reducing waste in current operations.

Use the Print ROI Summary button to generate a clean printout for stakeholder review.

The Generate Proposal tab creates professional, print-ready consulting proposals using the TLRW methodology framework.

Step 1: Fill in the business details including name, industry, revenue, primary challenge, employee count, and desired timeline.

Step 2: Click "Generate TLRW Proposal" to create the document.

Step 3: Review the generated proposal, which includes an executive summary, TLRW methodology breakdown, business assessment, deliverables, timeline, investment details, and credentials.

Step 4: Use the "Print Proposal" button to create a clean, professional printout. The proposal uses a white background with navy and gold branding for maximum readability.

Each proposal is customized based on the selected industry, challenge area, and company size to ensure relevant recommendations and deliverables.

Tabs not switching: Ensure JavaScript is enabled in your browser. Try refreshing the page.

API connections showing DEMO: The DEMO badge indicates that no live credentials have been saved. Enter valid API credentials and click Connect to switch to LIVE mode.

Print layout issues: For best results, use Chrome or Safari. Set margins to "Default" and disable headers/footers in the print dialog.

Data not persisting: This dashboard uses localStorage for credential storage. Clearing browser data will remove saved connections. Using private/incognito mode will not persist data.

CSV upload not working: Ensure your file is in .csv format with comma-separated values. The first row should contain column headers.

ROI calculator showing unusual numbers: Check that all input fields contain valid positive numbers. Percentages should be entered as whole numbers (e.g., 14.2 for 14.2%).

Business Intelligence Glossary

Key terms and definitions used throughout this dashboard.

Accounts Receivable (AR)
Money owed to a business by its customers for goods or services delivered but not yet paid for. AR is a current asset on the balance sheet and a key indicator of cash flow health. High AR relative to revenue may signal collection issues.
Break-Even Point
The revenue level at which total income equals total expenses, resulting in zero profit or loss. Understanding your break-even point helps determine the minimum revenue needed to sustain operations and informs pricing decisions.
Burn Rate
The rate at which a company spends its cash reserves, typically measured monthly. Burn rate is calculated as total monthly operating expenses. Combined with cash on hand, it determines your cash runway.
Cash Flow
The net amount of cash moving in and out of a business over a specific period. Positive cash flow means more money is coming in than going out. Cash flow differs from profit because it accounts for timing of actual payments, not just accrued revenue and expenses.
DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)
A measure of a company's ability to service its debt obligations. Calculated as Net Operating Income divided by Total Debt Service. A DSCR above 1.25x is generally considered healthy by lenders. Higher ratios indicate stronger ability to take on additional financing.
DUNS Number
A unique nine-digit identification number assigned by Dun & Bradstreet to each physical business location. Required for federal contracting, SBA loan applications, and business credit reporting. Obtaining a DUNS number is free.
EBITDA
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. A measure of core operational profitability that strips out financing costs, tax effects, and non-cash charges. Commonly used to compare profitability between companies.
Gross Margin
Revenue minus the direct costs of producing goods or services (Cost of Goods Sold), expressed as a percentage of revenue. Gross margin measures how efficiently a business converts revenue into profit before accounting for overhead expenses.
Line of Credit
A flexible financing arrangement with a bank that allows borrowing up to a set limit. Interest is only charged on the amount drawn, not the full limit. Lines of credit provide working capital flexibility for managing cash flow gaps and seasonal fluctuations.
Net Profit
Total revenue minus all expenses, including operating costs, interest, taxes, and depreciation. Also known as the "bottom line," net profit is the ultimate measure of a company's financial performance. Net profit margin shows this as a percentage of revenue.
P&L (Profit & Loss Statement)
A financial statement summarizing revenues, costs, and expenses over a specific period. Also known as an income statement. The P&L shows whether a business is making or losing money and is one of three key financial statements alongside the balance sheet and cash flow statement.
Runway
The amount of time a business can continue operating at its current burn rate before running out of cash. Calculated as total cash reserves divided by monthly burn rate. Maintaining 3-6 months of runway is recommended for small businesses.
SBA (Small Business Administration)
A United States government agency that provides support to small businesses through loan programs, counseling, contracting assistance, and advocacy. SBA does not lend directly but guarantees portions of loans made by partner lenders, reducing risk and enabling better terms for borrowers.
SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)
A federal program that encourages small businesses to engage in research and development with commercialization potential. SBIR grants are awarded in phases: Phase I ($250K for feasibility), Phase II ($1M for development), and Phase III (commercialization using non-SBIR funds).
TLRW Framework
NUVABASE's proprietary consulting methodology: Think, Learn, Reflect, Write. Think defines the strategic vision and goals. Learn gathers data and intelligence. Reflect analyzes findings to identify insights and opportunities. Write produces actionable plans, proposals, and implementation roadmaps. This structured approach ensures thorough analysis and measurable outcomes.
Working Capital
Current assets minus current liabilities. Working capital measures a company's short-term financial health and operational efficiency. Positive working capital indicates a business can cover its short-term obligations. Insufficient working capital is one of the most common reasons small businesses seek SBA financing.

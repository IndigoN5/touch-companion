Title 1 School Performance Dashboard — User Manual

Version 1.0 • NUVABASE Professional Consulting • Service to Service Group, Inc.

Getting Started

This dashboard helps Title 1 schools track student performance, attendance, interventions, parent engagement, and federal compliance — all in one place. No internet connection is needed after you open the file.

Tip: Open this file by right-clicking it and choosing "Open With" → Chrome (or your preferred web browser).

Tab-by-Tab Guide

1. Overview Tab

  • School Health Score: A single number (0–100) that combines reading, math, attendance, and chronic absenteeism into one overall grade for your school.
  • KPI Cards: The colored cards at the top show your key numbers — enrollment, attendance rate, reading/math proficiency, intervention count, and chronic absenteeism.
  • Charts: Bar charts show reading and math proficiency by grade level. The thin dark line on each bar is the target. Donut charts show budget allocation breakdown.

2. Academics Tab

  • Student Table: Lists every student with their reading level, math level, attendance, and intervention status.
  • Search: Type a student name, grade, or intervention name in the search box to filter the table.
  • Sort: Click any column header to sort the table by that column.
  • Color Coding: Green = On Track, Yellow = Monitor, Red = At Risk.

3. Attendance Tab

  • Shows school-wide attendance rate and breaks down chronically absent, at-risk, and satisfactory students.
  • The correlation chart shows how attendance directly impacts academic performance.

4. Interventions Tab

  • Tracks all active intervention programs (Reading Recovery, Math Mastery, etc.).
  • Shows which programs are working and which need review.
  • The table shows cost per student for each program.

5. Parent Engagement Tab

  • Logs all parent meetings, family nights, conferences, and surveys.
  • Tracks volunteer hours and survey response rates.
  • Required for Title 1 compliance reporting.

6. Compliance Tab

  • Checklist: A 22-item Title 1 compliance checklist based on ESSA requirements.
  • Green checkmarks = Complete, Yellow circles = Pending (with due dates), Red exclamation = Overdue.
  • Review this checklist before any audit.

7. Upload Data Tab

  • Step 1: Click "Student Data Template" to download a blank spreadsheet.
  • Step 2: Open it in Excel or Google Sheets, fill in your students, save as CSV.
  • Step 3: Drag the CSV file onto the upload area (or click to browse).
  • Step 4: Preview your data, then click "Confirm & Load."
  • Data saves in your browser automatically — it'll be there next time you open the dashboard.
Privacy: Your data never leaves your computer. Nothing is sent to the internet. 100% private and secure.

8. Data Entry Tab

  • Manually add individual student records or log parent engagement events.
  • Fill in the form fields and click "Save Record" or "Log Event."

Printing & Exporting

  • Click the "Export Report" button in the top header to print the dashboard.
  • When printing, choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog to create a PDF file.
  • The print layout automatically hides navigation and shows all tabs on one page.

Grade-Level Filtering

  • Use the grade dropdown in the header to filter all data by a specific grade (K through 5).
  • Select "All Grades" to see the complete picture again.

Troubleshooting

  • Dashboard shows code instead of visuals: Right-click the file → Open With → choose your browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari).
  • CSV upload not working: Make sure your file ends in .csv (not .xlsx). In Excel: File → Save As → CSV format.
  • Data disappeared: Data is saved per-browser. If you switch browsers or clear browsing data, you'll need to re-upload.
  • Charts look wrong: Refresh the page (F5 or Ctrl+R). If using uploaded data, the charts auto-calculate from your student records.

Glossary of Terms

Definitions of all terms used in this dashboard, organized by category.

Title 1 & Federal Terms
Title 1
A federal program (part of ESSA) providing financial assistance to schools with high percentages of low-income students to help all children meet state academic standards.
ESSA
Every Student Succeeds Act — the federal law (2015) governing K-12 public education, replacing No Child Left Behind. Sets requirements for accountability, testing, and Title 1 funding.
Free/Reduced Lunch (FRL)
A federal program providing free or discounted school meals to students from low-income families. FRL percentage is used to determine Title 1 eligibility.
Supplement Not Supplant
Federal requirement that Title 1 funds must add to (supplement), not replace (supplant), state and local funding. Schools cannot use Title 1 money for things they would have funded anyway.
Schoolwide Program
A Title 1 model where schools with 40%+ poverty can use Title 1 funds to improve the entire school program, rather than targeting individual students.
School-Parent Compact
A written agreement between the school, parents, and students outlining shared responsibilities for student achievement. Required by Title 1.
Comparability Report
Documentation proving that state/local resources are distributed equitably across Title 1 and non-Title 1 schools within a district.
Highly Qualified Teacher (HQT)
A teacher who meets state certification requirements, holds a bachelor's degree, and demonstrates subject-area competence.
Academic Terms
Proficiency
The percentage of students meeting or exceeding grade-level standards on state assessments in reading or math.
Above Grade Level
Student performing above the expected standard for their grade in a given subject.
At Grade Level
Student meeting the expected standard for their grade — performing where they should be.
Below Grade Level
Student performing below expectations. May benefit from intervention support.
Far Below Grade Level
Student performing significantly below expectations. Typically requires intensive intervention.
ELA
English Language Arts — the subject area covering reading, writing, speaking, and listening skills.
Growth (Grade Levels Gained)
The number of grade levels a student has progressed during the measurement period. A growth of +1.0 means one full year of progress.
Baseline
The starting measurement point against which progress is compared. Usually set at the beginning of the school year.
Attendance Terms
Attendance Rate
Percentage of school days a student was present. Calculated as: (Days Present ÷ Total Days) × 100.
Chronic Absenteeism
Missing 10% or more of school days (approximately 18+ days per year). A key federal accountability indicator under ESSA.
At-Risk (Attendance)
Students with 90–94% attendance rate — not yet chronically absent but trending toward it.
Intervention Terms
Intervention
A targeted program or strategy designed to address a specific academic or behavioral gap for struggling students.
Reading Recovery
An intensive short-term intervention for first-grade students who are the lowest-achieving in reading and writing.
RTI (Response to Intervention)
A multi-tier approach to early identification and support of students with learning and behavior needs.
Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3
Levels of intervention intensity. Tier 1 = whole class instruction. Tier 2 = small group targeted support. Tier 3 = intensive one-on-one intervention.
Dashboard & Analytics Terms
KPI
Key Performance Indicator — a measurable value showing how effectively a school is achieving key objectives.
School Health Score
A composite score (0–100) combining reading proficiency (30%), math proficiency (25%), attendance rate (25%), and inverse chronic absenteeism (20%).
Year-Over-Year (YoY)
Comparison of current year performance against the same metrics from the prior year.
CSV
Comma-Separated Values — a simple file format for spreadsheet data. Can be created from Excel or Google Sheets via "Save As → CSV."
Target Line
The thin dark vertical line on bar charts showing the goal or benchmark the school is aiming to reach.
TLRW Framework Terms
TLRW
Think, Learn, Reflect, Write — NUVABASE's proprietary four-phase decision and execution methodology with 12 quality gates (3 per phase).
Quality Gate
A checkpoint within the TLRW process that must be passed before moving to the next step. Each phase has 3 gates (12 total).
Triple Redundancy
The TLRW principle of running three independent analysis layers (surface, structural, systemic) to validate every decision.
Continuous Improvement
An ongoing process of evaluating and improving school performance using data-driven decision making. Core to both TLRW and Cognia accreditation.
